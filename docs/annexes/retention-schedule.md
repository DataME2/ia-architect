# Annex — Proposed Retention Schedule (Australia)

_[← Annexes](./README.md) · [EA home](../ea/README.md)_

A proposed per-record-class retention schedule realising **BR40**, drafted
against Australian requirements. It exists because BR40's status-based
rule — long for active Persons, a maximum of two years for inactive ones —
is **safe for some record classes and unsafe for others**, and a single
number cannot be right for all of them.

> **Not legal advice, and this one matters more than most.** These are the
> commonly applied Australian positions, not a ruling on this club's
> obligations. [Open question #30](../scope/open-questions.md) is the legal
> review, and **no disposal job should be built until it is answered.**

## The one thing to take away

**Retention belongs to the record class, not to the person.** Whether a
player is still active tells you when their *marketing preferences* can go.
It tells you almost nothing about when the record that they played in an
under-12 team in 2026, and who coached them, can go — and those two are
governed by completely different considerations.

BR40's two-year ceiling for inactive Persons is the risk in the current
rule. Applied to contact preferences it is generous. Applied to financial
records it likely breaches the ATO position. Applied to child-safety
records it is dramatically short of where child-serving organisations in
Australia have moved.

## Proposed schedule

| Record class | Proposed retention | Basis |
| ------------ | ------------------ | ----- |
| **Financial: invoices, payments, payment plans, vouchers, referee payments, remittances** | **7 years** from the end of the financial year the transaction falls in | The ATO requires business records be kept 5 years; incorporated associations and companies commonly work to 7 under their own governing legislation. 7 covers both without needing to distinguish |
| **Player registration and participation history** (who played, for which team, in which season) | **Until the Person turns 18, then a long tail — see the note below.** Not 2 years | This is the class where child-safety considerations dominate, not administrative convenience |
| **Working with Children Check verification records** (who held a clearance, its number, when it was verified, when it lapsed) | **7 years** after the Person ceases the child-related role, at minimum | Evidence that the club met its screening obligation at the time. The obligation is proved by the record, so disposing of it disposes of the defence |
| **Incident, injury, and complaint records involving a minor** | **Long tail — see the note below** | Limitation periods for child abuse claims in Queensland have been removed, and personal-injury clocks for minors typically run from age 18, not from the incident |
| **Consent records** (BR48, BR56, BR57) | **As long as the data the consent covers, plus the applicable evidence period** | A consent outlives its data by no useful margin, but disposing of it earlier leaves the club unable to show the processing was authorised |
| **Publicity consent** (BR57) | Current season, renewed each registration; **keep the record of the decision** for the evidence period even after the permission lapses | The permission is season-scoped by BR57. The *record that it was given or refused* is what answers a later complaint |
| **Employment and volunteer records** | **7 years** after the engagement ends | Fair Work employee-record requirements run to 7 years; volunteers are held to the same standard for consistency |
| **Contact details, communication preferences, marketing history** | **2 years** after the Person becomes inactive | This is the class BR40's two-year ceiling actually fits |
| **Operational ephemera** — availability responses, participation responses, notification logs | **2 seasons** | Useful for the current and prior season's patterns (BR12's decline-rate window), and of no value after |
| **Prospect records** (BR91–BR93) — the email and phone given at the demonstration door | **2 years** after the last visit, or immediately on request | Not a Person and not a club's data: this is Let'sDataTalk's own record of who looked at its product, so the club retention rules above do not reach it. Two years matches the marketing class one row up, for the same reason — a contact who has not looked in two years is a stale lead rather than a prospect. **A record with marketing consent is not thereby kept longer**: consent to be contacted is not a lawful basis to hold someone indefinitely, and deleting the row deletes the consent with it |

## The long tail, stated plainly

For records concerning **children in the organisation's care**, Australian
practice has moved a very long way from a few years. Queensland has
removed limitation periods for child sexual abuse claims, and the National
Redress Scheme's evidentiary expectations reach back decades. Child-serving
organisations increasingly retain participation, supervision, and screening
records for **45 years or longer** — the figure recommended to institutions
in the wake of the Royal Commission — precisely because the question "who
was supervising this child in 2026, and did they hold a clearance?" can be
asked in 2060.

**This is the single largest gap between BR40 as written and where a
football club with 800 juniors should probably sit.** It is not a detail
to settle later: a disposal job that removes participation records two
years after a child stops playing would destroy exactly the evidence the
club would need, and it would do so irreversibly.

The club's **insurer and Football Queensland's own affiliation
requirements** may also specify minimums, and both are cheaper to ask than
a lawyer.

## How this interacts with erasure (BR49)

The longer tails above are **named lawful bases for refusing an erasure
request**, which is what BR49 already provides for. Two consequences:

- For an active player, refusal becomes the common case rather than the
  exception — the refusal must still be recorded with its specific basis,
  and the requester told which class of record is retained and why.
- **De-identification is the release valve.** Where the club must retain
  the *fact* of participation but not the person's contact details, BR49's
  de-identify-rather-than-delete path satisfies both obligations. Most of
  the long-tail classes can be de-identified far earlier than they can be
  deleted, and designing for that is what keeps a 45-year retention from
  meaning 45 years of holding a phone number.

## What to do with this

1. **Take it to the legal review** ([#30](../scope/open-questions.md)) as a
   starting position rather than an open question — it is faster to correct
   a draft schedule than to commission one.
2. **Ask the club's insurer and Football Queensland** what they require.
   Free, fast, and frequently more specific than general law.
3. **Do not build a disposal job** until the schedule is confirmed. Building
   retention *tracking* is safe and useful; building automated deletion on
   an unconfirmed schedule is not.
