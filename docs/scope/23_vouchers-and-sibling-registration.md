# Project Scope — Vouchers, and the Family With Two Children

_[← Scope index](./README.md) · [EA home](../ea/README.md)_

**ArchiMate viewpoint:** Implementation & Migration.
**Delivered as:** branch `claude/identity-and-registration`.

Both halves of this come from the same population: **MiniRoos**. Most of
those families arrive with a government voucher, and most of them have more
than one child. Neither was handled, and one of them was quietly wrong.

## The bug: a parent with two children became two parents

`submit_public_registration` inserted the guardian unconditionally. A parent
registering a second child got a **second Person record**; a third child, a
third. Principle P1 says a guardian is one Person holding a role, and the
running system was manufacturing copies of the most common family shape in
the club.

It would not have stayed invisible — a guardian carries the placeholder date
of birth, so two copies share a name *and* a date of birth and BR5 flags them
as duplicates. That is the whole failure: **the club's reward for a family
having two children was a duplicate-resolution task**, generated by the
platform, once per sibling.

**BR80** fixes it at the source. The guardian is matched within the club on
normalised email before the insert; on a match the existing Person is reused.
That is not the silent merge BR5 forbids — nothing existing is combined, a
second copy is simply never created. Where no email is given the old
behaviour stands and BR5 does its job.

Email is the discriminator because a shared surname is not one: siblings and
step-families break name matching, and a parent typing their own address
twice is the strongest signal available without an external identifier
(BR44).

## Vouchers, and the rule that makes them safe

Most MiniRoos families have a Play On! voucher — the pilot club's
Committee-approved program (BR21). A registrar can now attach one, with its
PDF, and a club officer decides whether it is genuine.

**BR81 is the load-bearing part: attaching changes nothing.** A voucher in
`ATTACHED` reduces no balance. BR3 keeps failing, and under
[BR79](./22_no-pay-no-play.md) the player stays off the field — which is
the correct answer, and the one this initiative was asked to produce.

The reason is not caution for its own sake. A PDF in the system is a *claim*
that a discount applies, not the discount. A voucher that turns out to be
expired, already spent, or mistyped would otherwise have let a child take
the field on money the club never receives, and the club finds out when the
government refuses the reimbursement months later.

**Verification applies the value as an ordinary payment**, method `voucher`,
rather than as a discount column. That is what keeps the balance, the
receipts, BR3 and BR79 agreeing without anything reconciling them — and a
voucher later found invalid is reversed the same way any other receipt is
(BR77), not deleted.

The automatic check the club wants is [BR25](../ea/2_business/5_domain-context-and-rules.md),
already recorded: a code checked against the issuing government's own
interface, drafted by the Assistant and never decided by it (P3, decision 2).
None of the six state programs exposes one at this club's scale
([question #21](./open-questions.md)), so verification is a person looking at
a PDF, and the model is shaped so automation slots in as a *drafter* rather
than a rewrite.

## Plateaus

| Plateau | State |
| ------- | ----- |
| **Baseline** (before) | No voucher existed in the running system; a family's discount was a number a registrar typed into the balance, with no record of why. A parent of two children became two Person records |
| **Target** (delivered) | A voucher is attached with its document, sits visibly unverified, and moves money only when a club officer says so. A family registers a second child without retyping themselves, and the club holds them once. Proved across 17 further behavioural scenarios — 85 in total |

## Work packages and deliverables

### WP1 — BR80, at the source *(done)*

- **Deliverables:** the guardian match inside `submit_public_registration`;
  a *Register another child* step on the public form that carries the
  guardian's details forward and resets the child's.
- **Outcome:** one parent, one Person, however many children.

### WP2 — Vouchers *(done)*

- **Deliverables:** `registration_voucher` with its state machine and the
  `relief_payment_id` check constraint; `src/domain/finance/voucher.ts`;
  `src/data/vouchers.ts`; the panel on the registration detail.
- **Outcome:** attach, verify, reject — with the balance moving only on
  verification, and every decision audited with its author.

### WP3 — Storage *(done)*

- **Deliverables:** a private `vouchers` bucket; object paths of
  `<club_id>/<registration_id>/<uuid>.pdf`; read and write policies keyed on
  the first path segment; PDF-only, 5 MB cap, short-lived signed URLs.
- **Outcome:** the document is held where only that club can reach it.

### WP4 — Proof *(done)*

- **Deliverables:** `supabase/tests/15_vouchers_and_siblings.sql` — 17
  scenarios; 13 unit tests on the voucher domain.
- **Outcome:** verified to fail. Reverting the guardian match reports
  *"expected 1 shared guardian for two siblings, found 2 (BR80)"* and
  *"expected 1 person for the parent email, found 3 — the match is too
  strict"*.

## In scope / out of scope

**In scope:** club-side voucher attachment and manual verification; the PDF;
sibling registration.

**Out of scope:** the **claim** to government (BR23/BR24) — `CLAIMED` exists
as a state and nothing moves a voucher into it yet, because that is a CSV or
portal submission per program and belongs with the rest of C3. Also out:
automatic code verification (BR25), and voucher eligibility checking of any
kind — the club decides, the platform records.

## Gap notes

**The storage policies are not covered by CI, and this is the first
unproved thing in the slice.** The `storage` schema does not exist on the
local test Postgres, so the bucket and its policies are created behind an
`if exists` guard and `supabase/tests/*.sql` cannot exercise them. Every
other tenant-isolation claim in this project is proved behaviourally; this
one is asserted. `scripts/check_rls.py` was taught to recognise
`storage.objects` as a Supabase-owned table rather than being weakened —
the gate still fails on a real missing policy, which was verified.

**The family cannot upload the voucher themselves.** Attachment is a club
act, so a registrar keys in what the family emailed them. That is the
opposite of the collect-once goal, and it is deliberate for now: an
anonymous upload path is a new public write surface, and the one that exists
([decision 6](../decisions/6_public-registration-through-a-scoped-function.md))
took a whole initiative to get right. Raised as [#52](./open-questions.md)
rather than bolted on.

**Guardian matching is email-only.** A family who gives no email, or a
different email per child, still produces duplicates — caught by BR5, as
before. Widening the match would start guessing at identity, which is
exactly what BR5 exists to prevent.

**A voucher is attached to a registration, not to a Person.** A sibling with
their own voucher gets their own row, which is right; but a family voucher
covering two children has nowhere to go and would be entered twice, which
the single-use constraint would then refuse. No program in the reference set
works that way today, so it is recorded rather than modelled.

## Open questions

**#52 — Should a family attach their own voucher through the registration
link?** It is where the document actually is, and requiring a registrar to
collect and key every PDF re-creates the re-keying the slice exists to
remove. Against it: anonymous upload is a new public write surface, and
storage policies cannot check an invitation token the way the
`security definer` function can.

Resolves **[#51](./open-questions.md)** — whether BR79 benches a child while
a voucher claim is pending. It does not, because verification and claiming
are separate: relief applies the moment a club officer verifies the code, so
the family's balance clears then and the club carries the reimbursement risk
alone. That is the right way round — the club chose to accept the program
(BR21); the family did not choose the government's processing time.
