The method, walked

Guide

How a requirement becomes an implemented, documented change — and how an AI system fits into that as more than the tool executing it.

1. Walk the layers, top-down

Every change is checked against five layers, in this order, before any code is written. A layer with nothing to say still gets an explicit "no change" — silence isn't a decision.

#LayerAnswers
1Strategy & MotivationWhy does this exist? Who cares? Does it hold up against the project's principles?
2BusinessWho does what — people and AI agents — through which service and process?
3InformationWhat information exists, how does it flow and persist?
4ApplicationWhich software services and components realize the business services?
5TechnologyWhat runtimes, hosting, and CI/CD does it run on?

Full process: ea-first-change · notation and grounding rules: ea-doc-style

2. Actors: human, AI, and hybrid

A business actor no longer defaults to human by omission. Every actor states its kind — Human, AI, or Hybrid (a person and an AI sharing one role) — and an AI or Hybrid actor carries three more things: how much it can do without a human, exactly what it's authorized to decide, and who it hands off to when it can't.

Autonomy levels

How much an AI or hybrid actor may do before a human is involved. This site's Copilot sits at co-pilot.

Advisory Suggests; a human decides and acts.
Co-pilot this site Acts — drafts a complete change; a human reviews before it takes effect.
Autonomous with checkpoint Acts independently; a human is notified and can intervene after the fact.
Fully autonomous Acts independently; no routine human checkpoint.

Notation reference: ea-doc-style § Actors

3. Record why, not just what

A scope document explains an entire initiative's alignment across the layers. A decision record is smaller: one call — usually "why is this AI actor's autonomy set here and not somewhere else" — recorded once, linked from the row it explains, and never rewritten. A changed call gets a new record that supersedes the old one.

This site's own examples

Decision 1 — Copilot autonomy level: why co-pilot over advisory-only or fully autonomous.
Decision 2 — how this site renders its diagrams: why the pages use self-contained CSS instead of a diagramming library.
